## 1. Child safety standards page (new)

**File:** `app/web/child-safety.html` (11.8 KB)

### Why it was needed

Setting the app category to **Social** — which is the honest category for an app built
around a family tree, shared moments, comments, direct messages and polls — switched on
a Google Play requirement that does not apply to other categories:

> **Incomplete child safety standards declaration**
> All apps within social or dating categories must let us know how they comply with our
> child safety standards policy.

Play hard-blocks submission until this is satisfied, with the message:

> 1 issue affects all of your changes. Fix it to send changes for review.

The declaration demands a **Safety standards URL** — and its rules are specific:

- must link to standards against child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE)
- must be **active and publicly available anywhere in the world**
- must **not be a PDF**
- must **not be editable**

No such page existed. So the blocker was not a form to fill in — it required publishing
a new page on the public website first.

### Why it is a static file in `app/web/`

`buafamily.vercel.app` is the **Flutter web build** of the app, deployed by Vercel with:

- `buildCommand`: `bash tool/vercel_build.sh`
- `outputDirectory`: `build/web`

Flutter copies everything in `app/web/` into `build/web`, and Vercel serves real files
ahead of the `rewrites` catch-all. That is exactly how `privacy.html` and
`delete-account.html` are already served. Adding a third file to the same folder is the
established pattern here, needs no routing changes, and required no new deployment
setup.

### What the page says

Written to satisfy the declaration and to be genuinely accurate about the app:

- **Zero tolerance** statement on CSAE/CSAM
- The **specific prohibited behaviours**, named individually: sexual content involving a
  minor, grooming, sextortion, requesting or sharing CSAM, using the app to contact a
  child for sexual purposes, trafficking
- **Who the app is for** — adults 18+, admin approval required, limited to one family,
  not open to the public
- **How to report** — in-app reporting on moments, photos, comments, profiles and
  messages; admin removal of a member ends access immediately; a monitored email address
  as the designated child safety point of contact
- **What is done about reports** — remove content and account, preserve records, report
  to authorities, cooperate with law enforcement
- **Authority reporting** — NAPTIP and the Nigeria Police Force for a child in Nigeria,
  the relevant national authority elsewhere, plus international reporting bodies
- **Commitment** to comply with child safety laws and to review the standards

### How it was built

Deliberately matched to the existing legal pages rather than written fresh:

- the same CSS custom properties (`--green`, `--ink`, `--line`, …) and dark-mode block
- the same `header` / `h1` / `.card` / `.muted` structure
- the same **English + Hausa** toggle, wired to the **same `bua_legal_lang`**
  localStorage key, so switching language on one legal page carries over to the others

Hausa terminology reuses wording already established in `privacy.html` (`’yan uwa`,
`shugabanni`, `yara`, `manhaja`) instead of inventing new phrasing.

## 2. Play submission guide corrected

**File:** `docs/play-store/README.md`

The guide still described the app as having no in-app payments. That stopped being true
when **Korapay** payments for welfare-fund dues and causes were merged (`21cb5e8`,
"Pay dues and causes in the app through Korapay"). A submission guide that contradicts
the app is worse than no guide, because Play declarations get answered from it.

Six edits, CRLF consistency preserved, no bare LF:

1. **Financial features** — changed from "none" to **Other**, with the Korapay reality
   and the rule that keeps it exempt: this is a real-world payment into the family's own
   fund, **not** an in-app purchase, and Play Billing must not be used unless that
   changes
2. **Third-party processors** — added **Korapay** alongside Supabase, Firebase and
   Termii. Required for the "shared with third parties: No" answer to stay true
3. **Data safety** — financial row now covers dues and cause contributions paid through
   Korapay: amount, reference, gateway fee
4. **New note** — card and bank details live on Korapay's checkout and are never stored
   in the database, so they are correctly *not* declared
5. **New "Financial features declaration" section** — the tick-Other recommendation and
   its reasoning, a paste-ready description, a warning that step 2 asks for
   documentation, and the condition that collapses the exemption: **if paying ever gates
   a feature, Play Billing becomes mandatory**
6. **Both store descriptions** (English and Hausa) — the welfare-fund bullet now states
   that dues and causes can be paid in the app

## The blocker chain

Worth recording, because two of the three messages were misleading and cost real time:

| What Play said | What was actually wrong |
|---|---|
| "Add a full description to save" | The full description was fine (1,128/4,000 chars, verified through a reload). The store listing had an **unanswered AI asset declaration** on step 2, so the listing could not be committed, and the release reported the missing listing as a missing description |
| "Your app cannot be published yet. Complete the steps listed on the Dashboard." | Literal, and it resolved to two items: app category + contact details, and the store listing |
| "1 issue affects all of your changes" | The **child safety standards declaration** — triggered by the Social category |

The lesson: on Play, a release error often names the *symptom* on the release, while the
*cause* is a setup step elsewhere in the console. Reading the Dashboard and the
Publishing overview together is what resolved it.

## Side effects of this push

The Android workflow triggers on `push` to `master` with `paths: ['app/**', ...]`, and
this file lives under `app/`. So the push also starts **one CI build** (APKs + bundle) at
a new run number.

This is harmless — CI does not upload to Play — but note the version numbering split,
which is a pre-existing trap in this repo:

- **local builds** (`tool/build_android.ps1`) number by **git commit count** → produced `1.0.112`
- **CI builds** number by **run number** → will produce a different `1.0.x` for the same commit

`1.0.112` is the bundle uploaded to Play. A CI build from the same commit will *not*
carry the same version, so do not assume they match.
